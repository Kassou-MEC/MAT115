📐 The Unit Circle

What is the Unit Circle?

The unit circle is a circle with radius 1 centered at the origin (0, 0) of the coordinate plane. It's fundamental to understanding trigonometry because it relates angles to coordinates and trigonometric values.

📏 Angle Measurement

Degrees

A degree is 1/360 of a complete rotation around a circle.

  • Full circle: 360°
  • Straight angle: 180°
  • Right angle: 90°

Radians

A radian is the angle formed when the arc length equals the radius.

  • Full circle: 2π radians
  • Straight angle: π radians
  • Right angle: π/2 radians

Conversion Formulas

Degrees to Radians: radians = degrees × (π/180)

Radians to Degrees: degrees = radians × (180/π)

📊 Trigonometric Functions

Definition of Basic Trigonometric Ratios

In a right triangle with an angle θ:

Primary Trigonometric Functions

Sine (sin θ): sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse

Cosine (cos θ): cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse

Tangent (tan θ): tan θ = opposite / adjacent = sin θ / cos θ

Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions

Cosecant (csc θ): csc θ = 1 / sin θ = hypotenuse / opposite

Secant (sec θ): sec θ = 1 / cos θ = hypotenuse / adjacent

Cotangent (cot θ): cot θ = 1 / tan θ = adjacent / opposite

🔺 Right Triangle Trigonometry

Solving Right Triangles

To solve a right triangle means to find all unknown sides and angles. You need:

  • One angle (besides the right angle)
  • One side, OR
  • Two sides

Right Triangle Solver

Enter any two values (sides or one angle and one side):

Example 1: Given an Angle and a Side

Problem: In a right triangle, angle A = 35° and the hypotenuse = 10. Find the other sides.

Solution:

  • a = c × sin A = 10 × sin 35° ≈ 10 × 0.574 = 5.74
  • b = c × cos A = 10 × cos 35° ≈ 10 × 0.819 = 8.19
  • Angle B = 90° - 35° = 55°

Example 2: Given Two Sides

Problem: In a right triangle, a = 6 and b = 8. Find the hypotenuse and angles.

Solution:

  • c = √(a² + b²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 (Pythagorean Theorem)
  • tan A = a/b = 6/8 = 0.75, so A = arctan(0.75) ≈ 36.87°
  • Angle B = 90° - 36.87° = 53.13°

🔢 Pythagorean Identities

Pythagorean identities are fundamental relationships between trigonometric functions derived from the Pythagorean theorem applied to the unit circle.

Primary Pythagorean Identity

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Tangent-Secant Identity

1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

Derived by dividing the primary identity by cos²θ

Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

1 + cot²θ = csc²θ

Derived by dividing the primary identity by sin²θ

Proof of the Primary Identity

Consider a point (x, y) on the unit circle at angle θ from the positive x-axis:

  • By definition: x = cos θ and y = sin θ
  • The point lies on a circle of radius 1: x² + y² = 1²
  • Substituting: cos²θ + sin²θ = 1
  • Therefore: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 ✓

Applications of Pythagorean Identities

Example 1: Finding sin θ given cos θ

Problem: If cos θ = 3/5 and θ is in Quadrant I, find sin θ.

Solution:

  • Use: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  • sin²θ = 1 - cos²θ = 1 - (3/5)² = 1 - 9/25 = 16/25
  • sin θ = ±4/5
  • Since θ is in Quadrant I: sin θ = 4/5

Example 2: Simplifying using identities

Problem: Simplify sec²θ - tan²θ

Solution:

  • We know: 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
  • Rearranging: sec²θ - tan²θ = 1

🏔️ Applications: Angles of Elevation and Depression

Angle of Elevation

The angle of elevation is the angle formed by the horizontal line and the line of sight when looking UP at an object.

Angle of Depression

The angle of depression is the angle formed by the horizontal line and the line of sight when looking DOWN at an object.

Example 1: Angle of Elevation

Problem: A person standing 50 meters from the base of a building observes the top of the building at an angle of elevation of 60°. How tall is the building?

Solution:

  • We have: adjacent = 50 m, angle = 60°, find opposite (height)
  • tan 60° = height / 50
  • height = 50 × tan 60° = 50 × √3 ≈ 50 × 1.732 = 86.6 meters

Example 2: Angle of Depression

Problem: From the top of a lighthouse 120 feet high, the angle of depression to a boat is 15°. How far is the boat from the base of the lighthouse?

Solution:

  • Angle of depression = angle of elevation from boat to lighthouse = 15°
  • We have: opposite = 120 ft, angle = 15°, find adjacent (distance)
  • tan 15° = 120 / distance
  • distance = 120 / tan 15° ≈ 120 / 0.268 ≈ 448 feet

🔄 Trigonometric Functions of Any Angle

Trigonometric functions can be defined for any angle, not just acute angles. We use the unit circle and reference angles to find these values.

Quadrants and Signs

Quadrant I (0° to 90°)

All functions positive

sin > 0, cos > 0, tan > 0

Quadrant II (90° to 180°)

Only sine positive

sin > 0, cos < 0, tan < 0

Quadrant III (180° to 270°)

Only tangent positive

sin < 0, cos < 0, tan > 0

Quadrant IV (270° to 360°)

Only cosine positive

sin < 0, cos > 0, tan < 0

Memory Aid: "All Students Take Calculus"

  • All (Quadrant I): All functions positive
  • Students (Quadrant II): Sine positive
  • Take (Quadrant III): Tangent positive
  • Calculus (Quadrant IV): Cosine positive

Reference Angles

A reference angle is the acute angle formed between the terminal side of an angle and the x-axis.

  • Quadrant I: reference angle = θ
  • Quadrant II: reference angle = 180° - θ
  • Quadrant III: reference angle = θ - 180°
  • Quadrant IV: reference angle = 360° - θ

Example 1: Finding Trig Values for Any Angle

Problem: Find sin 210°

Solution:

  • 210° is in Quadrant III (180° < 210° < 270°)
  • Reference angle = 210° - 180° = 30°
  • In Quadrant III, sine is negative
  • sin 210° = -sin 30° = -1/2

Example 2: Using Reference Angles

Problem: Find cos 135° and tan 315°

Solution:

For cos 135°:

  • 135° is in Quadrant II
  • Reference angle = 180° - 135° = 45°
  • In Quadrant II, cosine is negative
  • cos 135° = -cos 45° = -√2/2

For tan 315°:

  • 315° is in Quadrant IV
  • Reference angle = 360° - 315° = 45°
  • In Quadrant IV, tangent is negative
  • tan 315° = -tan 45° = -1

Exercise Set 1: Evaluate Trigonometric Functions

Problem 1: Evaluate tan(5π/3)

Solution:

Step 1: Convert to degrees: 5π/3 × (180°/π) = 300°

Step 2: Determine quadrant: 300° is in Quadrant IV (270° < 300° < 360°)

Step 3: Find reference angle: 360° - 300° = 60°

Step 4: In Quadrant IV, tangent is negative

Step 5: tan(300°) = -tan(60°) = -√3

Answer: tan(5π/3) = -√3 ≈ -1.732

Problem 2: Evaluate sin(7π/4)

Solution:

Step 1: Convert to degrees: 7π/4 × (180°/π) = 315°

Step 2: Quadrant IV (270° < 315° < 360°)

Step 3: Reference angle: 360° - 315° = 45°

Step 4: In Quadrant IV, sine is negative

Step 5: sin(315°) = -sin(45°) = -√2/2

Answer: sin(7π/4) = -√2/2 ≈ -0.707

Problem 3: Evaluate cos(5π/6)

Solution:

Step 1: Convert to degrees: 5π/6 × (180°/π) = 150°

Step 2: Quadrant II (90° < 150° < 180°)

Step 3: Reference angle: 180° - 150° = 30°

Step 4: In Quadrant II, cosine is negative

Step 5: cos(150°) = -cos(30°) = -√3/2

Answer: cos(5π/6) = -√3/2 ≈ -0.866

Problem 4: Evaluate csc(3π/4)

Solution:

Step 1: Convert to degrees: 3π/4 × (180°/π) = 135°

Step 2: Quadrant II (90° < 135° < 180°)

Step 3: Reference angle: 180° - 135° = 45°

Step 4: In Quadrant II, sine is positive, so csc is positive

Step 5: sin(135°) = sin(45°) = √2/2

Step 6: csc(135°) = 1/sin(135°) = 1/(√2/2) = 2/√2 = √2

Answer: csc(3π/4) = √2 ≈ 1.414

Exercise Set 2: Using Pythagorean Identities

Problem 5: If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is in Quadrant II, find cos θ and tan θ

Solution:

Finding cos θ:

Use: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

(3/5)² + cos²θ = 1

9/25 + cos²θ = 1

cos²θ = 1 - 9/25 = 16/25

cos θ = ±4/5

Since θ is in Quadrant II, cos θ is negative

cos θ = -4/5

Finding tan θ:

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ = (3/5) / (-4/5) = -3/4

tan θ = -3/4 = -0.75

Problem 6: If tan θ = 2 and θ is in Quadrant III, find sin θ and cos θ

Solution:

Using the identity: 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

1 + (2)² = sec²θ

1 + 4 = sec²θ

sec²θ = 5

sec θ = ±√5

Since θ is in Quadrant III, sec θ is negative

sec θ = -√5

cos θ = 1/sec θ = -1/√5 = -√5/5 ≈ -0.447

Finding sin θ:

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

2 = sin θ / (-√5/5)

sin θ = 2 × (-√5/5) = -2√5/5

sin θ = -2√5/5 ≈ -0.894

Exercise Set 3: Right Triangle Applications

Problem 7: A ladder 20 feet long leans against a wall. If the ladder makes an angle of 65° with the ground, how high up the wall does the ladder reach?

Solution:

Given:

  • Hypotenuse (ladder) = 20 feet
  • Angle with ground = 65°
  • Find: height (opposite side)

Using sine:

sin(65°) = height / 20

height = 20 × sin(65°)

height = 20 × 0.906

Answer: The ladder reaches approximately 18.13 feet up the wall

Problem 8: From a point 100 meters from the base of a tower, the angle of elevation to the top is 28°. Find the height of the tower.

Solution:

Given:

  • Adjacent side (distance) = 100 meters
  • Angle of elevation = 28°
  • Find: height (opposite side)

Using tangent:

tan(28°) = height / 100

height = 100 × tan(28°)

height = 100 × 0.532

Answer: The tower is approximately 53.2 meters tall

Challenge 2: If sin θ = -0.6 and tan θ > 0, find the exact value of cos θ

Solution:

Step 1: Determine the quadrant

sin θ < 0 means Quadrant III or IV

tan θ > 0 means Quadrant I or III

Therefore, θ is in Quadrant III

Step 2: Use Pythagorean identity

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

(-0.6)² + cos²θ = 1

0.36 + cos²θ = 1

cos²θ = 0.64

cos θ = ±0.8

Step 3: Apply quadrant rule

In Quadrant III, cosine is negative

Answer: cos θ = -0.8 or -4/5

Challenge 3: A surveyor stands 250 feet from a building. The angles of elevation to the top and bottom of a flagpole on top of the building are 42° and 38° respectively. Find the height of the flagpole.

Solution:

Step 1: Find height to top of flagpole

tan(42°) = h₁ / 250

h₁ = 250 × tan(42°) = 250 × 0.9004 ≈ 225.1 feet

Step 2: Find height to bottom of flagpole (top of building)

tan(38°) = h₂ / 250

h₂ = 250 × tan(38°) = 250 × 0.7813 ≈ 195.3 feet

Step 3: Find flagpole height

Flagpole height = h₁ - h₂ = 225.1 - 195.3

Answer: The flagpole is approximately 29.8 feet tall
